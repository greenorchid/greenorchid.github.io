# Saffron & Clementine Almond Cake

A wonderfully moist, naturally gluten-free cake combining the bright citrus flavour of whole clementines with the delicate floral notes of saffron.

Inspired by a traditional whole-citrus almond cake, this version uses a carefully measured quantity of cooked clementine purée to ensure a consistent texture, with additional sugar for balanced sweetness.

A light clementine syrup is brushed over the warm cake, enhancing its citrus flavour while keeping the crumb beautifully moist.

The cake is delicious on its own but particularly good served with lightly sweetened mascarpone cream or crème fraîche.

## Instructions

### Prepare the Clementines

1. Place approximately 450g of whole, unpeeled clementines in a saucepan and cover with cold water.
2. Bring to a boil, reduce the heat and simmer gently for 1½–2 hours, until the fruit is completely soft. Top up the water as necessary.
3. Drain thoroughly and allow the clementines to cool.
4. Cut the fruit in half, remove any seeds and blend the entire fruit, including the peel and pith, until smooth.
5. Weigh out exactly **320g of cooked clementine purée**. If the purée is excessively watery, gently reduce it in a saucepan before weighing.
6. Allow the purée to cool completely before using.

### Prepare the Saffron

7. Lightly crush the saffron strands using a pestle and mortar or the back of a spoon.
8. Add 2 tablespoons of boiling water and leave to infuse for 15 minutes.
9. Allow the saffron infusion to cool to room temperature.

### Make the Cake

10. Preheat the oven to **160°C fan / 180°C conventional**.
11. Grease and line a **20cm round cake tin**, preferably a springform tin at least 7cm deep.
12. In a large mixing bowl, combine the eggs and 250g caster sugar.
13. Whisk using an electric mixer for 4–5 minutes, until pale, thickened and slightly aerated.
14. Gently fold in the cooled clementine purée.
15. In a separate bowl, combine the ground almonds, gluten-free baking powder and a pinch of salt.
16. Gradually fold the almond mixture into the egg mixture, taking care not to knock out too much air.
17. Fold in the cooled saffron infusion until evenly incorporated.
18. Pour the batter into the prepared cake tin and gently level the surface.

### Bake

19. Bake for approximately **50–65 minutes**, checking after 45 minutes.
20. If the top begins to brown too quickly, loosely cover with aluminium foil.
21. The cake is ready when the edges are set, the centre feels gently springy and a skewer inserted into the middle comes out without wet batter.
22. If using a food thermometer, aim for a centre temperature of approximately **96–98°C**.
23. Remove from the oven and leave the cake in its tin while preparing the syrup.

### Prepare the Clementine Syrup

24. Combine the fresh clementine juice, 40g caster sugar and lemon juice in a small saucepan.
25. Heat gently over medium-low heat, stirring until the sugar has completely dissolved.
26. Simmer for 1–2 minutes, then remove from the heat.
27. While the cake is still warm, gently prick the surface with a skewer.
28. Brush or spoon the warm syrup gradually over the cake, allowing each addition to absorb before adding more.
29. Avoid saturating the cake, as the almond and clementine crumb is already naturally moist.
30. Leave the cake to cool completely in its tin before carefully removing.

### Prepare the Mascarpone Cream (Optional)

31. Place the mascarpone, icing sugar, clementine zest and vanilla extract in a mixing bowl.
32. Beat briefly until smooth.
33. Gradually add the cold double cream while whisking on low speed.
34. Increase the speed slightly and whisk until soft peaks form.
35. Avoid overwhipping, as mascarpone can become grainy.
36. Refrigerate until ready to serve.

### Serve

37. Transfer the cooled cake to a serving plate.
38. Dust lightly with icing sugar if desired.
39. Decorate with fresh clementine slices, zest or edible flowers.
40. Serve with a generous spoonful of mascarpone cream.

## Tips

- **Weigh the purée:** Always use 320g of cooked clementine purée rather than relying solely on the starting weight of the fruit. This helps control moisture and prevents a wet, sunken centre.
- **Drain thoroughly:** After boiling, allow the clementines to drain completely. Excess water can compromise the cake's structure.
- **Use the correct tin:** A 20cm round tin, at least 7cm deep, produces the best balance of height and even baking.
- **Avoid overmixing:** Once the ground almonds are added, fold gently to preserve the air incorporated during whisking.
- **Don't overbake:** The cake should remain moist and tender. A slightly damp crumb is normal, but the centre should not contain wet batter.
- **Control the syrup:** Add the syrup gradually. You may not need all of it if the cake is already particularly moist.
- **Saffron infusion:** Crushing and steeping the saffron before adding it ensures a more even flavour and colour throughout the cake.
- **Make ahead:** This cake is particularly good the following day, once the citrus and saffron flavours have developed.
- **Storage:** Store in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. Bring to room temperature before serving.
- **Freezing:** The cake can be frozen without the mascarpone cream for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly and defrost overnight in the refrigerator.
- **Gluten-free:** Naturally gluten-free when prepared with certified gluten-free baking powder.
- **Serving suggestion:** Fresh clementine segments and lightly sweetened mascarpone cream complement the citrus and floral flavours beautifully.
